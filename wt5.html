Sports Illustrated Tickets

Even though I was still working under Tyger Shark this term, the entirety of my time was spent working on a development team of Sports Illustrated Tickets. Thus I am designing the work term report around SI Tickets rather than Tyger Shark.

Employer

Sports Illustrated Tickets is a fairly new branch of the Sports Illustrated brand, and an up-and-coming competitor to platforms like TicketMaster. During my time there, the platform has gained lots of momentum and official sports partners including the NFL and MLB! Although it's still new, the recognizeable brand name of Sports Illustrated helped it soar straight to the competitors' level in the ticketing landscape.

Job Roles - Development Engineer Intern
Automated Pricer - App Features

At the beginning of my term, I was moved from my data role to yet another new development team. This time, I worked as a more full-stack developer on an automated-pricing tool. This tool pulled from multiple different external sources, including competitor pricing, weather, and past events' pricing. It would then use all of these variables, as well as adjustments made by the person using the tool, to output an optimal price for each ticket for any given event.

There were 3 primary things I worked on. The first was creating a settings page to be able to tweak certain values that drive the pricer, such as how aggressive we wanted the pricing to be per-event. I also worked closely with the main user of this tool to refine trend charts and reporting of how well the tickets are selling.

And finally, my major project that lasted half the term was creating an end-to-end flow to allow the user of the tool to create and monitor pricing experiments for events. This includes creating multiple different pricing strategies, which are configurations of pricing settings. Then the user assigns sections of event stadiums to each pricing strategy when creating their experiment. And finally from there, the user can monitor how each pricing strategy performs day-by-day as the experiment continues. This was a pretty cool project to own. Not only did I get to own the entire flow, but it was around this time that the company started leaning heavily into AI development. On a personal note, I'm not too fond of the AI future we are heading towards, but professionally, gaining experience working with Claude code heavily was very valuable experience to gain. The codebase I worked in was heavily Claude-oriented, consisting of CLAUDE.md files in nearly every directory, as well as having many Claude skills. Leveraging this, I was able to work on multiple tickets at once, sometimes having up to 4 in-progress at once. I accomplished this by learning about git worktrees, and having a Claude agent working in each one. Learning about all of this and becoming proficient enough to make a few skills of my own made the last couple months of my term pretty exciting and fresh!

Automated Pricer - NextJS Development

Another new aspect of my work this term was working in a NextJS framework rather than the Node.js framework I'm used to. This was a prety interesting and eye-opening approach to the client-server model that I haven't been exposed to before. It was pretty interesting that the structure of both the backend API endpoints and the frontend website paths were fully driven by the directory structure of the codebase. I found that there were both benefits and drawbacks to both approaches. On one hand, it made it much more straightforward to add new endpoints and pages, removing the need to 'register' them in a sort of central 'routes' file. But on the other hand, it made the codebase structure much more challenging to navigate and quickly read through because there are so many layers of nested directories that it's hard to get a comprehensive mental picture of the codebase.

Goals
Goal #1 - Professional and Ethical Behaviour

My first goal was that I would like to improve my ability to fill idle time during my day. There were many times where I found myself in between two tasks and unsure what I could be doing while waiting for my next one.

I think I did a pretty good job accomplishing this goal. The last month of this term saw a shift in my workflow from majority-manual coding to allowing Claude agents to take over. Before this, I did a decent job filling my idle time not by making new features, but by moving through the application and finding small improvements that could be made, and creating tickets for them. After moving to Claude, I filled my idle time even better, not only continuing to explore the features I made and finding improvements, but I also created two Claude skills that ended up becoming standard practice for my team to use. I created them without being asked simply when I felt they were needed for my flow to become smoother.

Goal #2 - Communication

This was a continuation from my communication goal of last term:
I want to continue to improve my confidence to express my opinions on various topics during meetings or times of planning.

I believe I did a pretty good job of this goal too. The manager I was working under was very approachable and valued my opinion, so it was very easy to feel comfortable sharing my thoughts and ideas. He set up weekly one-on-one meetings to touch-base and check in on me. Numerous times I had real and purposeful questions and suggestions to bring up. Additionally, about halfway through the term, I was given ownership of an entire end-to-end feature flow. Thus, naturally, the users of the playform and my feature had questions and feedback, and I would have consistent back-and-forths with them and was not just a fly on the wall in these meetings.

Goal #3 - Backend Literacy

This goal was also a continuation of my backend goal of last term:
I want to obtain a lot of hands-on experience with writing server-side code. After my last term, I am very comfortable working in react frontend, but a lot of backend programming is still pretty foreign to me. I would love to achieve a more deep understanding through a great project working in the server environment (Eg. connecting to external apis, creating complex endpoints, etc).

I think simply due to more exposure, I have successfully become more accustomed to the backend and using APIs. I didn't do any *major* backend-related tasks, but I created a number of endpoints this term and have overally just become a bit more comfortable and understanding of the way backend and using APIs work. I did learn something brand new this term, which was working in a Next.js architecture and seeing how that differs from Node.js. Next.js was quite interesting in that the directory architecture of the codebase was what directly drove the shape and structure of the APIs and the frontend routes. This was an interesting new way of code organization that I found had both pros and cons in my opinion. On one hand, creating new API endpoints and frontend pages became a lot easier to do with less coding overhead: it's as easy as making one file with a specific name. However, in my opinion, I found it harder to navigate and comprehend where exactly specific files lived in the codebase. But regardless, learning a second distinct method of code organization also helped with my comfort-level with server-side code.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this as a very interesting and unique last term. Even though I worked at the same plce as last term, I still had a completely distinct experience due to both working in a new NextJS framework, as well as learning the ins and outs of leveraging an AI coding agent in Claude Code. This was a great term to end on that gave me a lot of helpful insights into the technologies of the future.
